# Mila 1.9.2-beta.3

- **If you installed beta 2, dictation and recording could capture nothing at all — that's fixed.** On some Macs the microphone indicator flickered rapidly, the recording came out empty, and transcription then failed with "Failed to decode audio", which looked like the transcription server was down when it wasn't. The recovery added in beta 2 was reacting to a change it had caused itself, restarting the microphone about seven times a second so no audio was ever captured. Beta 2 has been withdrawn; this build replaces it. Mila now also refuses to send an empty recording for transcription, and tells you no audio was captured instead of showing a server error you can't act on.
- **Unplugging your headphones mid-meeting no longer silently stops the recording.** If the microphone Mila was recording from went away — USB EarPods or a wired headset unplugged, a Bluetooth headset switching profile, another app taking the device — macOS quietly swapped in a different mic and Mila's capture died on the spot. The timer kept counting and the recording looked normal in the list, but nothing after that moment was actually recorded: one user's 26-minute Zoom call was saved as 24 seconds of audio. Mila now notices within a few seconds and picks the recording back up on whichever microphone is available, so you lose a moment instead of the meeting.
- **App audio (the other side of a meeting) comes back on its own if macOS drops it.** When the system audio capture died part way through a recording, Mila silently continued with just your own microphone. It now restarts that capture and writes down what happened, so a half-captured meeting is something you can see rather than something you discover in the transcript.
- **Recordings show how long they actually are.** A recording's length came from the timer that ran while you recorded, so a capture that ended early still claimed the full length — a 24-second file listed as 26:24. The length now comes from the audio itself.
- **AI setup is now two tabs instead of one crowded one.** **Settings → AI Provider** is where you pick Claude, Cursor or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint once, and **Settings → AI Features** is where you turn on what Mila does with it — automatic summaries, suggested recording names, your own Send-to-LLM prompt, and Live AI — each an expandable row with its own switch and prompt. Everything you had configured carries over untouched. Output language moved to the AI Features tab, since it governs summaries too, not just Live AI.
- **Live AI now tells you when it can't run.** The switch is greyed out with the reason when your setup doesn't support it — previously you could turn it on, record, and get nothing back.
- **Every Settings tab is visible again.** The window was too narrow for its own tab bar, so the last tab collapsed into a `»` menu and looked disabled. The window is wider, and the Updates settings moved into a new **General** tab rather than having a tab of their own.
- **Beta builds can no longer reach you if you didn't ask for them.** A pre-release published without its channel marker was offered to everyone on 30 July. Mila now refuses a pre-release version outright unless you've ticked "Receive beta versions of Mila".
- **Self-hosted Hebrew servers get the English model filled in for you.** If your transcription endpoint runs an ivrit.ai model, Mila pre-fills the separate English model field instead of leaving it blank, so English recordings stop coming back with Hebrew words spliced in. Clear the field by hand and Mila leaves it cleared.
